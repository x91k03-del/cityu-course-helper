2         GE1126: Smart Consumers ‒ Think like Lawyers
Part II Course Details
Abstract
As the course name suggests, this course aims at providing legal knowledge to students to help them become smart
consumers using a highly interactive and innovative Team Based Learning (“TBL”) approach.  Through team-based
role-playing, team-based discussion of current issues concerning consumer rights and real consumer disputes and video
viewing with appropriate use of AI, students will discover the legal issues and principles that govern everyday consumer
transactions.
Moving entirely away from traditional, passive lecture models, this course implements an innovative gamiﬁed and
collaborative learning environment. Students will actively master the IRAC (Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion) legal
reasoning system to construct airtight legal arguments. By synthesizing team-work with structured logical thinking,
students are dynamically trained to deconstruct complex factual scenarios, isolate core liabilities, and apply statutory law to
real-world disputes.
Discussion of current issues and real cases with a team-based approach will encourage students to think about how "real"
consumer disputes are dealt with and to help students consider whether the victims and their families can have any legal
claim against the beauty service provider.  The lecturer will also review with students common terms and conditions of
diﬀerent types of consumer agreements, e.g. for internet services or the usage of Facebook.
Hence, students will know the "dos" and "don'ts" whenever they enter into a consumer transaction.  They will also become
more aware of current issues involving consumer rights and trained to be more inquisitive.
This course aims to provide participants with:
• an insight into the basic legal principles governing consumer transactions and how such knowledge can help protect
their interests as consumers;
• training in basic and advanced legal reasoning skills (speciﬁcally the IRAC system), logical thinking, critical analysis, and
approaches to resolving consumer disputes;
• an awareness of current issues involving consumers' rights and common consumer pitfalls in various types of
consumers transactions;   
• an insight into the current consumer protection framework and mechanism in Hong Kong;
• training in research and presentation skills as well as oral and written arguments throughout;
• communication skills and team-work and cooperation through structured team learning structures

Additional Information for ATs
To pass the course, students need to obtain an overall total mark of 40% and a minimum of 30% in both the overall
continuous assessment and examination components.
Mid-term essay assessment ‒ A real-life legal problem will be given to students and each student will need to write and
provide an advice to the client giving and explaining legal issues and principles that they have learnt and present their
ﬁndings and argument in written form.   The essay must be explicitly structured around the IRAC formatting framework,
demonstrating sound logical progression and deductive analysis.
A group research and presentation task: Students are required to do research and give a presentation on common consumer
pitfalls and current regulations and agencies in Hong Kong which provide protection and assistance for consumers in one
of the following areas: (i) beauty services; (ii) travel services; (iii) insurance services; (iv) banking and ﬁnancial services; (v)
money lending services; (vi) health club services; (vii) Chinese medicine practice; or (viii) product safety.
6         GE1126: Smart Consumers ‒ Think like Lawyers
Students are also required to produce an "end-product" relating to their research topic (e.g. a poster or documentary) to go
with their presentation.  Teams will be expected to run an interactive segment (e.g., debate, audience-polling, or simulation)
to demonstrate team-learning and engagement mechanics.
The lecturer will have conferences with individual groups to provide guidance on doing this project and some background
materials on the research topics. The task will be assessed by the tutors.
Examination: The duration of the examination may last for 2 hours and it will be at the discretion of the course leader.
The course leader will announce the duration of the examination in the ﬁrst week of the course.  The exam problems will
heavily prioritize logical analytical thinking, testing students' ability to rapidly and accurately deploy the in-depth IRAC
structure under timed conditions to solve convoluted commercial disputes.
